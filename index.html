Our name, our method

Every job starts
with advice.

Core Access starts every job with advice, not equipment. First we ask whether the work at height can be eliminated, then whether a safer method can replace it. Only when neither is practicable do we engineer safe access: a designed scaffold with protection built in.

The Core

  1. 1Eliminate

    We advise whether the task can be done without working at height — from the ground, or by pre-assembly.

  2. 2Substitute

    If height can’t be avoided, we advise a safer method or equipment, even if it isn’t ours.

  3. 3Engineer

    Only then do we step in: a layout drawing to the Singapore codes, system scaffold (ringlock or aluminium mobile tower), with guardrails, toe boards, boarded platforms and ties built in.

Core Access: eliminate first, substitute next, engineer the rest.

The order follows the upstream risk controls of Singapore’s Code of Practice on WSH Risk Management and ISO 45001 — going beyond the Bangladesh minimum.

Singapore standards

Designed, calculated and certified
to Singapore standards.

Singapore runs one of the most tightly regulated scaffold and work-at-height regimes in the region. We apply its codes of practice and standards as our benchmark on every project in Bangladesh — on top of Bangladesh law and each client’s own HSE rules.

Legislation & codes of practice

WSH Act 2006Workplace Safety and Health Act — risk management framework
WSH (Scaffolds) Regulations 2011Design, erection, inspection and use of scaffolds
WSH (Work at Heights) Regulations 2013Planning, permits and fall prevention for work at height
CP Working Safely at Heights 2013Code of Practice for Working Safely at Heights
SS 659:2020Code of practice for scaffolds

Scaffold material & equipment standards

SS 280-1:2006Metal scaffoldings — frame scaffoldings
SS 280-2:2009Metal scaffoldings — modular scaffoldings
SS 311:2005Steel tubes and fittings for tubular scaffolding
BS EN 12811-1Temporary works equipment — scaffolds: performance requirements and general design
BS EN 1004Mobile access and working towers (aluminium towers, as referenced by SS 659)
SS EN 13374:2018Temporary edge protection systems

Fall-protection standards

SS 528 Parts 1–6Personal fall-arrest systems — harnesses, lanyards, energy absorbers, self-retracting lifelines, connectors and system tests
SS 570-1 & -2:2022Single-point anchor devices and flexible horizontal lifeline systems
SS 541:2008Restraint belts
SS 607:2015Design of active fall-protection systems

Structural design codes

SS EN 1993Design of steel structures
EN 1999Design of aluminium structures

Engineering calculations

Nothing is erected without a calculation. Every design package is prepared to the codes above and issued for client and consultant approval.

  • 01Dead, imposed and wind loads for each lift and bay
  • 02Member, coupler, connection and tie checks
  • 03Base plate, sole board and ground-bearing checks
  • 04Stability checks for towers and cantilevered sections
  • 05Calculation report, general arrangement drawings and tie layout
  • 06Full engineered design wherever Singapore rules require one — scaffolds over 30 m high, more than four persons per bay or heavy-duty loads, and all cantilevered systems

Remote certification from Singapore

Our Singapore core engineering checks, signs and issues design certificates remotely — so Bangladesh sites get Singapore-level sign-off without waiting for an overseas visit.

  • 01Design certificate issued digitally before erection
  • 02Site changes re-checked and re-certified remotely
  • 03Inspection on completion, at least every 7 days and after bad weather, recorded in a scaffold register
  • 04Full record pack at handover — calculations, drawings, certificates and inspection register — ready for audit
  1. Step 1CalculateLoads, members, ties and bearing
  2. Step 2CheckIndependent review by Singapore engineering
  3. Step 3CertifySigned design certificate issued digitally
  4. Step 4RecordInspections and register through to handover

Standards are applied at their current editions. Bangladesh law — including the Bangladesh National Building Code and the Bangladesh Labour Act — always applies on Bangladesh sites.
